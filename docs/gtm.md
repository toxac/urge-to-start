# Go-To-Market

### Phase 0: The "Empty Storefront" Strategy (Pre-Launch)
*Don't build a landing page that asks for emails. Build a landing page that asks for **problems**.*

- **The Tactic:** Launch a brutally simple microsite (or even a Typeform/Google Form) called **"The Friction Log."** 
- **The Guerrilla Twist:** It has zero branding about the app. It just says: *"What frustrated you today? Describe the moment. Be specific."* 
- **The Amplification:** Print out QR codes for this log on cheap sticker paper. Go to co-working spaces, university libraries, and post offices. Stick them next to broken printers, slow elevators, or long queues. The caption: *"Sick of this? Tell us exactly why."* 
- **The Goal:** You aren't collecting leads; you are collecting **raw market data** (Opportunity > Idea). You use this data internally to build your first few "Quests" (e.g., we found 40% of people are frustrated by client onboarding, so our first Quest is "The Onboarding Audit").

---

### Phase 1: The "Open-Source Sprint" (The Digital Guerrilla)
*Don't sell the app. Sell the 7-day challenge. The app is just the reward for finishing.*

- **The Tactic:** Launch a public **"7-Day Opportunity Sprint"** on LinkedIn, Reddit (r/entrepreneur, r/sideproject), and Indie Hackers. **No paid ads.** 
- **The Guerrilla Twist:** It runs entirely via a public Twitter/X thread or a free WhatsApp/Discord community. Every day, you post *one* task from the app (e.g., Day 3: *"Audit your 9-to-5 job. List 3 things your boss does that wastes time. That is a potential business."*).
- **The Amplification (Referral as a Ribbon):** To access Day 4's task, participants *must* post their Day 3 findings publicly and tag 2 friends who "complain about the same thing." This is viral by design, not by vanity metrics, but by *homework*. People share because it makes them look smart and action-oriented.
- **The Hook:** At the end of the 7 days, they are invited into a private "Urge Cohort" (your early access list). **They earned** the right to use the app; they didn't sign up for it.

### Phase 2: The "Anti-Launch" Event (Physical Guerrilla)
*Don't host a webinar. Host a "No-Pitch Night."*

- **The Tactic:** In your nearest major city, rent a cheap public space (a park, a library meeting room, a backroom of a pub) for **"The Urge Intervention."** 
- **The Guerrilla Twist:** The rules of entry are strict. 
  1. No laptops allowed.
  2. You are banned from saying your business idea.
  3. You must bring **one physical object** that represents a friction point in your daily life (e.g., a tangled charging cable, a confusing receipt, a broken coffee lid).
- **The Flow:** Everyone puts their object in the center of the room. Attendees pick up an object that *isn't* theirs and have 5 minutes to pitch a business model based on *that* object's problem. 
- **The Amplification:** Film the chaos. Post 60-second reels/shorts with the caption: *"We didn't talk about funding once. We just solved problems."* This visually proves your philosophy *instantly* to a global audience, costing you $0 in ad spend.

---

### Phase 3: The "Build in Public" Ambassador Loop
*Don't hire influencers. Convert your first 50 users into "Tinkerers."*

- **The Tactic:** Your first 100 app users don't get "early adopter" badges. They get a physical, high-quality **patch or pin** (cheap to manufacture) mailed to them that says "Urge Tinkerer - Class 001."
- **The Guerrilla Twist:** To earn the physical patch, they don't just use the app. They must complete their first **MSP (Minimum Sellable Product)** Quest and post a screenshot of their first $1 earned or their first "No" from a customer. 
- **The Amplification:** These Tinkerers become your salesforce. Give them a unique "Friction Finder" URL. When they share their journey on socials, they aren't marketing Urge; they are marketing *their own business*. Urge is just the toolkit they used. This attracts purely high-intent users who see the app as a means to an end (a business), not a distraction.

---

### Phase 4: The "Graveyard of Ideas" (Sustained Digital Guerrilla)
*Don't blog about trends. Blog about failures.*

- **The Tactic:** On your website's blog (or a LinkedIn newsletter), create a weekly column called **"The Morgue."**
- **The Guerrilla Twist:** You don't feature successful Urge users. You feature *ideas that died*. You interview users who realized their "opportunity" wasn't real after doing the Quests. You celebrate the speed of their failure.
- **Why this works:** In an ecosystem obsessed with unicorns, celebrating the pragmatic death of a bad idea is radical. It builds immense trust. It proves you aren't selling snake oil. This content is pure SEO gold and shareable ammunition for skeptics.

---

### Phase 5: The "MSP Bounty" (The Growth Loop)
*Don't ask for reviews. Ask for receipts.*

- **The Tactic:** Inside the app, after a user completes a "Sell" Quest, ask them a single question: *"Who did you sell to?"*
- **The Guerrilla Twist:** Offer no monetary reward. Instead, offer a **"Public Roast"** or **"Public Cheer"** session in your community group. If they share the email/name of the person they sold to (anonymized, of course), you, as the founder, will personally write a thank-you note to *that customer* on behalf of the entrepreneur, praising them for taking a chance on a new builder.
- **The Amplification:** That customer is so blown away by the gesture that they post about it. You now have a marketing loop that bypasses the entrepreneur entirely and goes straight to the *end-user*—perfectly aligning with your "Customer at the Core" philosophy.

---

### Summary of the "Minimum Marketing" Checklist:
| Do This | Not This |
| :--- | :--- |
| Print QR stickers for "Friction Logs" | Facebook/Instagram Ads |
| Run a 7-day public LinkedIn challenge | Influencer sponsorships |
| Host a "No-Pitch Night" in a pub | Expensive launch party with speakers |
| Mail physical patches to first 100 users | Email drip campaigns with discounts |
| Publish a "Graveyard of Ideas" blog | Press releases to TechCrunch |
