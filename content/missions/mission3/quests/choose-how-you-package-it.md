When you notice a real market problem, your immediate human instinct is to jump straight to a single product format. If you know how to write software code, you immediately visualize building an app. If you are naturally good at organizing workflows, you immediately visualize selling a guidebook or setting up a course. 

This deep instinct to lock into a vehicle too early blinds you to better alternative pathways that might be much faster and cheaper to test. A single, validated problem can be packaged and delivered across multiple business shapes. 

Take a time-strapped local business owner who hates doing basic administrative invoicing work. You could solve that exact problem for them in at least four genuinely different ways:
*   **Done-For-You Service:** You act as a manual assistant or agency and handle their data entry and invoicing for a flat project fee.
*   **Handy Digital Tool:** You build a simple app or automation script that handles their invoice data transfers automatically.
*   **Community Board or Directory:** You build a portal that connects these business owners directly with pre-vetted virtual assistants who do the work.
*   **Manual or Guidebook:** You sell a clear template manual or instructional guide on how to configure invoicing using tools that already exist in the market.

Same problem. Completely different execution pathways. One requires you to write custom code, one requires you to manage operations, one requires you to match people, and one requires you to teach. 

Review how your target project shapes across all four models, evaluate your personal advantages, and lock in the format you will use to make your very first sale.
