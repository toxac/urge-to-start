If you are building a local service or a small digital tool to cover your basic living expenses, you can skip this section entirely and go straight to mapping out your first customers. However, if you are looking at your project and wondering, "How massive could this actually get in the future?", you need a realistic way to calculate your ceiling.

In the business world, this is called mapping your total market size. The biggest trap is looking at global, generic numbers (like reading that the global software market is worth billions) and assuming you can easily capture a fraction of it. That is fiction. 

To map out a realistic ceiling, we look at the market from the top down across two layers:
*   **The Global Category Ceiling:** The total amount of money being spent on your entire business category worldwide. If you are building a school scheduling tool, this is the total revenue generated by all school software globally. This is a massive, theoretical pool, but it tells you if you are entering an expanding industry or a shrinking pond.
*   **Your Serviceable Segment:** The global pool filtered down to the specific boundaries of your business shape. This means filtering by your immediate geography, language, and precise demographic tier. If your tool is only built for English-speaking, secondary-level institutions, your serviceable segment is a fraction of the global pool. This is your real, long-term playground.

Mapping these parameters doesn't change how you make your first sale next week. But it prevents you from committing months of your life to a market that is fundamentally too tiny to ever grow past a local hobby.
