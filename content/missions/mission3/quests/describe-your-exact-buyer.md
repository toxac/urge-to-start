A broad target description leads directly to a failed, generic launch. Stating that your solution is simply for "busy college students" or "small business owners" is just listing a broad demographic category. It is a census bucket, not an actionable target market. 

To design an offer that instantly commands attention and cuts through the noise, you must get hyper-specific about who your buyer is on paper, and what is happening contextually underneath their daily lives. Demographics explain who your buyer is external to your product, but psychographics explain exactly why they will pull out their wallet to buy it. 

Consider two people who are the exact same age, live in the same high-end neighborhood, and earn the exact same annual salary. On a generic demographic spreadsheet, they look like identical data points. However, one spends their weekends volunteering at a local animal shelter and buying organic, sustainably sourced goods. The other spends their free time at high-speed motor racing tracks and purchasing the latest cutting-edge technology. They do not experience pain points the same way, and they do not buy the same products.
