Your business does not trade in features; it trades in value. The biggest trap early builders fall into is talking endlessly about *what* their product does, rather than *why* it matters to the person buying it. 

Your value proposition is the unique blend of speed, convenience, or cost savings that makes your buyer's biggest daily headache disappear. If you are building a tool that cuts a restaurant manager's inventory tracking time from four hours down to thirty minutes, your product isn't an inventory tracker. Your value proposition is handing three and a half hours of personal freedom back to a stressed manager every single week.

You also need to clarify how you will treat those buyers once they arrive. Your customer relationship style dictates how you build trust and stop people from leaving. Are you offering a highly manual, high-touch personal service via direct message, or are you creating an automated, hands-off setup where users onboard themselves?
