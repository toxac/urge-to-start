The people in your immediate circles—your friends, your classmates, your team members, or your co-workers—are telling you what they would pay to fix every single day. They are just calling it complaining. Strangers will be polite to you, but the people you know well complain honestly. 

Most people tune out casual complaints or offer a quick bit of advice to make the conversation comfortable. The habit you need to build instead is intense curiosity. When someone expresses frustration, don't try to resolve it for them—try to understand it. Ask why it's hard. Ask what they've already tried. 

Watch your active watering holes closely for these three signals:
*   **The Sigh:** A sound of tired acceptance about a recurring chore. They have completely stopped expecting the process to get better.
*   **The Clumsy Hack:** The Post-it note taped to a monitor, the messy group chat pinned to the top, or the manual spreadsheet holding a data loop together. A hack is absolute evidence that a gap exists and that they are already spending daily effort to patch it.
*   **The "I Wish":** When someone explicitly states: *"I wish someone would just handle this."* That isn't a casual comment; that is a direct business request.

Select the core groups you interact with most frequently and log two explicit moments where you caught someone using a clumsy shortcut to finish a job.
