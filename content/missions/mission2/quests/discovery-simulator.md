Before you go out and talk to real buyers in the open market, you must learn to ask questions that don't invite confirmation bias. If you ask someone: *"I have an idea for an app that fixes this—would you use it?"*, they will almost always say yes to be polite or encourage you. The founder builds it based on that feedback, but nobody buys it. That fails because you're asking someone to predict their own future behavior, which humans are genuinely terrible at. 

What they tell you about their **past behavior** is completely reliable. The core rule of running an un-biased interview is simple: don't ask anyone whether your idea is good. Ask them about their life.

*   *"Would you pay for a dog-walking service?"* is a future guess. They will guess generously because it sounds nice.
*   *"When was the last time you paid someone to walk your dog?"* is a historical fact. Either they have or they haven't. If they have never looked for it, never paid for it, and never tried to solve it, they are not your customer.
